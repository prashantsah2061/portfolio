About Me

I'm a Computer Science student at Caldwell University, double minoring in Mathematics and Business Analytics. My work sits at the intersection of machine learning, cognitive science, and data analysis, where I study how intelligent systems represent, retrieve, and reason over information across time.

"I like turning complex questions into careful experiments, readable code, and clear conclusions."

Through undergraduate research, I have worked on long-range retrieval in sequence models and LLM working-memory evaluation, comparing architectures such as LSTMs, Transformers, and Mamba. I enjoy the full research loop: reading papers, designing experiments, building tools, analyzing results, and communicating what the evidence actually says. My long-term goal is to pursue a PhD in Computer Science and contribute to more reliable, interpretable, and efficient AI systems.

Research Experience

Sept 2026 – Present
Research Assistant
Caldwell University · Advisor: Prof. Bella Veksler
Do Large Language Models Know When Their Working Memory Fails?
Investigating LLM working-memory performance and metacognitive awareness using verbal and spatial n-back tasks. Reviewing cognitive science and machine learning research to identify limitations in existing LLM working-memory evaluations. Designing experiments to evaluate accuracy, confidence calibration, and performance across increasing memory demands, with an evaluation framework using Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Brier Score.
Sept 2025 – Aug 2026
Undergraduate Research Assistant
Caldwell University · Advisor: Prof. Vlad Veksler
Scaling Long-Range Retrieval: A Comparison of LSTM, Transformer, and Mamba on a Needle-in-a-Haystack Task
Developed a synthetic Needle-in-a-Haystack task to compare long-range retrieval abilities of LSTM, Transformer, and Mamba models. Evaluated performance across sequence lengths from 32 to 1,024 tokens using PyTorch. Compared retrieval accuracy, training time, and memory usage, and observed Transformer retrieval accuracy decline from approximately 99% at shorter sequences to 19% at 1,024 tokens.
